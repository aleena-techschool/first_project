Ecosystems

Ecosystems are composed of a variety of biotic and abiotic components that function in an
interrelated way. [84] The structure and composition is determined by various environmental
factors that are interrelated. Variations of these factors will initiate dynamic modifications to the
ecosystem. Some of the more important components are soil, atmosphere, radiation from the
sun, water, and living organisms.[85]

Central to the ecosystem concept is the idea that living organisms interact with every other
element in their local environment. Eugene Odum, a founder of ecology, stated: "Any unit that
includes all of the organisms (i.e.: the "community") in a given area interacting with the physical
environment so that a flow of energy leads to clearly defined trophic structure, biotic diversity,
and material cycles (i.e.: exchange of materials between living and nonliving parts) within the
system is an ecosystem. "[86] Within the ecosystem, species are connected and dependent upon
one another in the food chain, and exchange energy and matter between themselves as well as
with their environment. [87] The human ecosystem concept is based on the human/nature
dichotomy. and the idea that all species are ecologically dependent on each other, as well as
with the abiotic constituents of theirbiotope.[88]

A smaller unit of size is called a microecosystem. For example, a microsystem can be a stone
and all the life under it. A macroecosystem might involve a whole ecoregion, with its drainage
basin. [89]

Ecosystem - key Points

  • An ecosystem is a community of living organisms and their non-living environment.
  • It includes biotic components such as plants, animals, bacteria, and fungi.
  • It also includes abiotic components such as sunlight, air, water, soil, and temperature.
  • Producers (mainly green plants) make their own food.
  • Consumers depend on other organisms for food.
  • Decomposers break down dead plants and animals and return nutrients to the soil.
